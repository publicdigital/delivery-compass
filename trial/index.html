The Gov Delivery Library · what it holds, as of 15 October 2026

843,253 words of rules for delivering things in UK government

If you are running a project, programme or service in central government, this is the guidance someone may hold you to. It is published by 16 different bodies, across 297 web pages and PDFs, with no single owner and no single place it all lives. Read end to end at an average pace, it would take about 56 hours: a week and a half of doing nothing else.

The Gov Delivery Library lets your AI assistant search all of it for you. Ask a question in plain English and the answer quotes the guidance, links to the exact heading, gives the page date, and warns you where a newer document has overtaken an older one.

Add it to your assistant

Three steps: add it, check it works, then ask it a real question. The library works as a connector (an MCP server) that your assistant can call while it answers you.

1. Add it

Every client needs the same address:

https://publicdigital-delivery-compass.netlify.app/mcp?key=gdl-trial-2026

! WarningCheck your department's rules before you connect any tool to a work account.
Claude (web, desktop and mobile)
  1. In Claude on the web or desktop, open Settings, then Connectors.
  2. Choose Add custom connector.
  3. Name it Gov Delivery Library and paste the address.
  4. If Claude asks how to sign in, choose no sign-in. The key in the address is all it needs.
  5. Select Add.

If you see "Couldn't register with Gov Delivery Library's sign-in service", delete the connector and add it again from scratch. Trying to reconnect the failed one keeps failing.

Works on every plan. The free plan allows one custom connector. On Team and Enterprise plans an owner has to add it for the organisation first. Once added on the web, it is also available in the mobile app.

ChatGPT
  1. Open Settings, then Apps & Connectors, then Advanced settings, and turn on Developer mode.
  2. Back in Apps & Connectors, choose Create.
  3. Name it Gov Delivery Library, paste the address, set authentication to No authentication and confirm that you trust it.

Needs a paid plan. On Business, Enterprise and Edu workspaces an admin may need to allow developer mode first. OpenAI moves these menus often, so the names may differ slightly.

Microsoft 365 Copilot

You cannot add a connector to Microsoft 365 Copilot yourself. Your IT team can add it as a custom MCP connector in Copilot Studio or the Microsoft 365 admin centre, using the address above. Many departments will not do this for a trial, and that is a reasonable answer.

2. Check it works

Start a new chat and ask:

Using the Gov Delivery Library, what does it cover?

A good answer shows that your assistant used the Gov Delivery Library, lists its sources by area (assurance, digital, funding and so on), and says what it does not cover, such as NHS guidance. If your assistant says it has no such tool, check the connector is switched on for that chat.

3. Ask it a real question

Start every question with “Using the Gov Delivery Library”. Without those words, assistants usually search the web or answer from memory, even with the library connected. In our own tests, Claude used the library on 1 of 5 plain questions, and on every question that named it.

Any wording works as long as it names the library: “Using the Gov Delivery Library, …” or “What does the Gov Delivery Library say about …?”. Try one of these:

  • Using the Gov Delivery Library, do I still need GDS approval for digital spend?

    This one shows where a newer document has overtaken an older one.

  • Using the Gov Delivery Library, what does the Green Book say about optimism bias?

    This one quotes a “must” word for word.

  • Using the Gov Delivery Library, what happens at a Gate 2 review?

  • Using the Gov Delivery Library, do I need a business case for a £3m digital project?

  • Using the Gov Delivery Library, how should we evaluate an AI pilot?

02 · Who writes it

Sixteen publishers, none of them in charge of the whole.

HM Treasury owns the money, NISTA owns assurance, GDS owns the digital spine, and Government Project Delivery owns the profession's standard. Then a long tail of departments, functions and task forces publish the pieces that sit in between.

03 · How much of it is a rule

10,067 statements telling you what to do. 958 of them say you must.

Every sentence that tells a reader what they must, should, may or could do has been pulled out and graded by its wording. That is how the library can show you the mandatory lines word for word rather than in summary. The grading is done by machine from the verb used, so treat these counts as a good estimate, not a legal audit.

04 · How it fits together

One document leads to another. Sometimes they disagree.

Government publishes documents, not the links between them. These 58 links were mapped by hand: which document expands on which, which one you need before another applies, which assurance gates sit on which decisions, and where a newer document has quietly overtaken an older one.

Live example

Digital spend controls changed on 1 April 2026. Four older documents still describe the old process. The Service Manual says the controls "were replaced on 1 April 2026", while the Technology Code of Practice, the DDaT Playbook, the agile business case guidance and Gate Review 4 still point readers at the old central approval route.

05 · How current it is

It keeps moving. 26 of these documents changed this year.

Each dot is a document, placed at the date its publisher last updated it and sized by its length. The library checks every source weekly and records the publisher's own change notes, so a two-year-old copy on a shared drive is easy to spot.

The Teal Book publishes no page dates, so it is left off; the library compares its text week by week instead. For the Service Manual, the dot shows its most recently updated page of 143.

06 · The edges

What it holds, and what it doesn't.

Linked, not reproduced (8)

Registered and pointed to, but the text is not in the library. Answers say so rather than guess.

Out of scope by design

If a question is about one of these, an empty answer does not mean no rule applies.

  • NHS-specific guidance (for example DTAC, DCB0129, DCB0160, the NHS service standard)
  • Guidance from the governments of Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland
  • Procurement law, including Procurement Act 2023 guidance
  • Functional standards other than GovS 002 (Project Delivery) and GovS 005 (Digital)

Before you rely on it

  • It is unofficial. It is not a government service, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by HM Treasury, NISTA, GDS or any other publisher listed.
  • It quotes the guidance and links to the original. The original is the authority, so follow the link before you act on anything important.
  • Your assistant writes the final answer, and assistants can still get things wrong. The library gives it the exact text and dates to work from.
  • The library only receives the search your assistant sends it, never the rest of your conversation. We keep those searches, with personal details such as email addresses and phone numbers removed, so we can see what people need and where the library has gaps. They are deleted when the trial ends and never shared. Leave personal details out of your questions anyway.
  • The text comes from GOV.UK and projectdelivery.gov.uk under the Open Government Licence v3.0.
  • Public Digital, where I work, is part of the delivery consortium for Cabinet Office's Test, Learn and Grow programme. The programme's playbook is one of the sources here, quoted and ranked like every other.
