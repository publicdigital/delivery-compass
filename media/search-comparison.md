# Same question, with and without the library

What Claude.ai said to the five test questions, asked plainly and then asked to use the
Gov Delivery Library. Taken from Oli's by-hand checks on 29 October (old tool
descriptions) and 31 October 2026 (new descriptions). For planning the like-for-like
videos: which questions show the biggest difference, what to scroll to, and what the
captions could say.

Claude's answers change every time, so treat these as what to look for, not a script.
Do a dry run before recording and check the difference still shows.

## 1. Do I need a business case for a £3m digital project?

**Plain, 29 October: from memory, no search.**
- Said "At £3m, expect a full case, not a short one."
- Said digital projects "may also need spend controls approval. This is often at a much lower threshold than £3m." That stopped being true on 1 April 2026.
- No links at all.

**Plain, 31 October: searched the web.**
- Knew the spend controls changed on 1 April 2026, but said "I could not find the new thresholds and process."
- Sources: Inside Housing, a Gardiner market intelligence page, a DfE cyber security page, and the GOV.UK spending controls page.

**With the library (both days).**
- Quoted the mandatory line word for word: "Departments must develop spending proposals in the form of business cases" (Treasury Approvals Process).
- Said there is no fixed £3m threshold: your department's delegated authority limit decides who approves.
- Said the case can be smaller: a single-stage Business Justification Case may be enough, but "You must not use a BJC if the proposal is novel, contentious or repercussive."
- Gave the current route: tell your assurance team as soon as possible, and you need Treasury approval above your limit or for novel, contentious or repercussive proposals.
- Flagged that the £10m figure in older agile guidance conflicts with the Service Manual, and that the AI Playbook's £100k and £1m figures may be out of date.

**What to show:** in the plain answer, stop on "expect a full case" or "I could not find the new thresholds and process" and the housing magazine link. In the library answer, stop on the quoted "must", then on the Business Justification Case line, then hover over a gov.uk link.

**Caption ideas**
- "Asked plainly: a guess, and a housing magazine."
- "With the library: the current route, the rule quoted, and a smaller option."

## 2. What happens at a Gate 2 review?

**Plain, 29 October: searched the web.**
- How the review runs came partly from the Northern Ireland Department of Finance and a GOV.UK Word document: a planning meeting two weeks before, three or four days long, a report on the last day.
- Asked whether you meant an NHS England or client stage-gate instead.

**Plain, 31 October: from memory.**
- Said Gate 2 "comes after the business case is approved".
- Described interviews, an oral readout and a red-to-green rating, with no sources.

**With the library.**
- 31 October: "It also corrects part of my first answer." Gate 2 follows Gate 1 and tests the Outline Business Case, not an approved one.
- Listed what the review assesses: viability, potential for success, value for money, the proposed approach, and readiness to go to market.
- A project normally has one Gate 2, before any commitment to suppliers.
- Said plainly that the library sections it found do not cover the review days, interviews or ratings, so "Treat that part of my first answer as unverified."
- 29 October: gave the full checklist of what the review team checks, grouped by area.

**What to show:** the plain answer's "after the business case is approved", then the library answer's "Corrections to my first answer" heading.

**Caption ideas**
- "From memory: confident, and slightly wrong."
- "With the library, Claude corrects itself, and says what it can't verify."

## 3. Do I still need GDS approval for digital spend?

**Plain, 29 October: searched the web.**
- Right on the headline: GDS no longer approves digital spend, and the controls changed on 1 April 2026.
- Listed only two of the three reasons you still need Treasury approval: novel and contentious.

**Plain, 31 October: searched the web.**
- Right again, and better sourced: the Service Manual, a PublicTechnology article and the GDS roadmap.
- Warned that some GDS pages still show the old £100,000 and £1 million thresholds.
- Did not list the three Treasury approval reasons.

**With the library (both days).**
- All three reasons for Treasury approval: novel, contentious and repercussive, with an example of each.
- Three warnings that older documents still describe the old process:
  - the Technology Code of Practice spend controls section, marked superseded since 1 April 2026
  - HM Treasury's agile business case guidance, which still has Cabinet Office spend controls releasing Beta funding and a £10m figure
  - NISTA Gate Review 4, which the library says conflicts with the Service Manual, and it cannot tell which is right
- The current page's date: 1 April 2026.

**What to show:** the library answer's warnings section. This is the library's distinctive feature: a newer document has overtaken an older one, and it says so.

**Caption ideas**
- "Both say no. Only one warns that three other documents still say yes."

## 4. How should we evaluate an AI pilot?

**Plain (both days): from memory.**
- Sound general advice: set a baseline, agree success criteria in advance, measure use and outcomes, count the full cost, decide whether to scale, change or stop.
- No government sources.

**With the library.**
- Found the Evaluation Task Force's guidance on evaluating AI interventions (advisory, part of the Magenta Book family).
- Evaluate in stages: a formative evaluation after each test stage, a summative one at full roll-out, and repeat it because AI tools change over time.
- Build a full theory of change, including unintended effects and different effects on different groups.
- AI makes controlled trials easier, because you can control who gets the tool and when. If outcomes are uncertain, use theory-based methods such as outcome harvesting.
- From the DDaT Playbook: plan the pilot before procurement, write breakpoints into the contract, and beware of "pilot creep".
- Flagged that the DDaT Playbook page dates from 2023.

**What to show:** the staged evaluation lifecycle and "pilot creep" lines, with their links.

**Caption ideas**
- "Plain: good advice. With the library: what government guidance actually asks for."

## 5. What does the Green Book say about optimism bias?

**29 October:** Claude used the library on the first try, without being asked. It quoted the mandatory line ("Practitioners must account for optimism bias by making explicit adjustments at the outset of an appraisal") and gave the page date (5 February 2026). The follow-up found the digital-specific figures, from the Digital and Data Benefits framework: reduce benefits by 54% and increase costs by 200% when you have no data of your own.

**31 October:** Claude searched the web, read the Green Book 2026 PDF itself, and was accurate. With the library, it confirmed the answer and dismissed a commentator's claim that reference class forecasting replaces optimism bias.

**Why it is not for the video:** a question that names one specific document is one the web can answer. The digital-specific 54% and 200% figures are the one thing the plain answer lacked, if you want a short extra clip.

## Before recording

- Use an incognito chat if the connector is available there. In one 29 October follow-up, Claude mentioned "your NHS work" from memory.
- Make sure the plain question really is plain. Do not start a chat by naming the library and then ask the plain version in the same chat.
- Some answers end by offering more help ("Which setting are you in?"). Stop the clip before that, or crop it.
