# Governing an accelerator

**Draft, 2026-10-07. Not published, not in the corpus, and not in Public Digital's
house voice yet.** Written because a tester went looking for this and found nothing.

This is deliberately outside `sources/`. Delivery Compass quotes guidance and never
writes it; the interface says so, and that promise is the reason anyone should trust
what it shows. Authored material must not be mixed in where a reader could mistake it
for something HM Treasury published. If this becomes a Public Digital piece, it lives
on public.digital and the tool links to it like any other source.

---

## The gap, stated precisely

Test, Learn and Grow is a £100m, three-year Cabinet Office programme launched in July
2025. It runs Accelerators on named missions: AI in frontline services, violence
against women and girls, economic inactivity. In June 2026 a further £10m Capability
Fund was announced for up to twenty locations.

Search GOV.UK for what has been published about it and you get three privacy notices.

That is the whole public record of how these things are governed. The data protection
documentation exists because the law required it. Nothing else was required, so
nothing else was written.

This is not a complaint about secrecy. It is an observation about sequencing: the
programme is running ahead of any published account of how to run one, which means
every new Accelerator, and every one of those twenty funded locations, works it out
again from scratch.

## What already exists, so we do not rewrite it

Four things cover part of the ground. Anyone writing this should start by reading
them rather than inventing a fifth framework.

**HM Treasury, Test and Learn** (May 2026, published alongside the Magenta Book).
Covers what test and learn means, why it matters, how it supports robust evaluation,
how to prioritise where to use it, and how to implement it across four stages:
Explore, Co-design, Test, Grow. Section 6.2, "Adapting governance for Test and Learn",
is the closest thing that exists to the subject of this piece. It is good, and it is
three paragraphs long.

**GDS, Governance principles for agile service delivery** (2016). Decisions at the
right level, do not slow down delivery, go see for yourself, do it only if it adds
value. Ten years old, still correct, written for a service team rather than a
cross-government mission.

**Behavioural Insights Team and Nesta, Test and learn: a playbook for mission-driven
government** (April 2025). The intellectual groundwork for the programme.

**The Magenta Book.** Evaluation, thoroughly. Answers what counts as evidence; does
not answer who decides, or how fast.

What none of them does is govern an *Accelerator*: a time-boxed, cross-organisational
team, centrally funded, working on a mission problem in a place, expected to stop
things that do not work and scale things that do.

## Where it collides with the standing framework

The interesting part is not that guidance is missing. It is that the existing
framework actively pulls the other way, and nobody has written down how to resolve it.

**Accountability assumes one organisation.** GovS 002 requires a Senior Responsible
Owner who is accountable for the project. An Accelerator spans departments and a local
place. Whoever is named SRO is accountable for delivery they do not control. In
practice this gets resolved by a board, and boards are where accountability goes to
become everyone's.

**Treasury approval assumes fixed scope.** The Treasury Approvals Process is built
around a business case that firms up through SOC, OBC and FBC. An Accelerator's entire
value is that the scope is a question, not an answer. The agile business case
clarification helps, but it was written for digital projects with a known service to
build, and its thresholds changed when spend controls were replaced on 1 April 2026.

**Gate reviews assume a line.** NISTA's Gate Reviews 0 to 5 run from Strategic
Assessment to Operations Review. They are a good instrument pointed at a different
shape of work. An Accelerator that runs four parallel experiments and kills two does
not have a Gate 3.

**The Orange Book minimises risk.** Test and learn requires taking bounded risk
deliberately. These are reconcilable, but only if someone states the risk appetite for
experimentation explicitly, and says who owns it. Nobody has.

**Nothing in the framework is good at stopping.** Government governance is built to
keep things on track. An Accelerator needs a credible, unembarrassing way to conclude
that an intervention does not work, say so publicly, and reallocate the money. That is
a governance design problem, not a cultural one.

## What the guidance should answer

A first cut at the table of contents, written as the questions a team actually has in
week one.

1. **Who is accountable, and for what?** A named SRO for the Accelerator as a vehicle,
   distinct from accountability for any intervention it tests. The distinction matters:
   the vehicle succeeds when it produces reliable answers, including negative ones.
2. **What is the funding shape?** How money is committed, over what period, what
   triggers the next tranche, and what happens to unspent funds when something stops
   early. Stopping early must not be financially punishing, or nothing will stop early.
3. **What replaces the gates?** Decision points defined by evidence rather than
   calendar: what has to be true to move from Explore to Test, from Test to Grow, and
   what evidence is sufficient at each. Magenta Book standards apply, proportionate to
   the decision being made.
4. **Who can stop it, on what basis, and how fast?** Including the team itself. A stop
   decision should be reportable as a result, not a failure.
5. **What is the risk appetite, and who set it?** Written down, specific to this
   Accelerator, signed by someone.
6. **What assurance applies?** Whether NISTA integrated assurance is in scope, what a
   proportionate alternative looks like, and who decides. An Accelerator spending
   several million is not a major project but is not nothing either.
7. **How does the place share control?** Where a local partner co-delivers, what they
   decide, what they are accountable for, and what happens at the end of the funding.
8. **What gets published, and when?** Including results that did not work. The privacy
   notices show the programme can publish when obliged to; the question is what it
   publishes when it is not.
9. **How does an intervention graduate?** The handover from Accelerator to a permanent
   owner is where this will most often fail. Who receives it, with what funding, under
   what standard.

## How to write it

Three things would make this land rather than join the pile.

**Ground it in the standing framework rather than beside it.** Every section should
say which existing requirement it satisfies, adapts, or disapplies, and on whose
authority. Guidance that ignores GovS 002 and the Treasury Approvals Process will be
ignored in turn by the people who have to satisfy them.

**Write it from the Accelerators that are running.** Three exist. Their actual
governance arrangements, good and bad, are better material than any model. Public
Digital is in the delivery partnership and has access to that material.

**Publish the negative cases.** The most useful section will be the one describing an
intervention that was stopped, what the evidence was, and how the decision was taken.
Nobody publishes those, which is precisely why nobody knows how to do it.

## Honest caveats

The gap is real for *Accelerator governance*. It is narrower than "test and learn
governance has not been written", because HMT wrote a chapter on that in May 2026 and
a well-informed person in this field had not seen it. That discoverability problem is
its own finding, and arguably the more urgent one: guidance that exists but cannot be
found fails in the same way as guidance that was never written.

Public Digital is part of the consortium delivering this programme. Anything published
should say so plainly. Writing the governance guidance for a programme you are paid to
deliver is defensible and useful, but only if the interest is declared.
